Kaluach Help

Locations and Settings

Location is more than a city label: it affects astronomy, regional calendar behavior, candle lighting, and the preferred zmanim method.

Saved locations

Previously saved places appear in their own scrolling list in Location Settings. After loading one, you can revise its fields or assign a different zmanim method before saving. To remove a place from the list, click the trash icon at the end of its row, or right-click it and choose Delete. If it is the location in use, the zmanim keep using it until you choose another.

Saved locations follow you through iCloud to your other Macs and to iKaluach; the location currently in use stays separate on each device.

Zmanim shita

Each location has its own zmanim method: one of the built-in profiles — Eretz Yisrael (GRA), Eretz Yisrael (M”A), Diaspora (GRA), Diaspora (M”A), Chabad, and the community methods — a custom profile, or Show All.

Check Also show Tzeit Shabbat / Chag by Rabbeinu Tam to add Rabbeinu Tam’s end of Shabbat and Yom Tov after the method’s own — for communities with followers of both. It has no effect when the method already uses Rabbeinu Tam, or with Show All, which lists every opinion anyway.

Israel and diaspora

Kaluach normally derives Israel-or-diaspora behavior from the selected location. If map data classifies a place incorrectly, the Country control can override that classification for the saved location without changing its time zone.

Time zone and daylight saving

A searched location or Use My Location stores a named time zone, allowing the correct daylight-saving rule to be applied for each date. Manually entered coordinates trigger the same lookup after you press Return or Tab. Places in Judea and Samaria (and Gaza) set to Israel use Israel’s clock, so daylight saving starts and ends on Israel’s dates there too.

Leave Daylight Saving on Automatic for normal use. If a rare location remains consistently one hour wrong, you can force daylight saving on or off for that saved location.

General preferences

Settings include English or Hebrew display, 12- or 24-hour time, visible holiday groups, the preferred opening month display, On opening, show — Today, or the Last selected day on this Mac — and the historical Julian/Gregorian transition used for displayed civil dates. They also hold Zmanim Alarms and the Event reminder time — see Alarms and reminders. At the bottom, a line shows whether Kaluach is syncing with iCloud. Settings themselves stay on each device.

Kaluach starts in Hebrew or English, whichever comes first in your Mac’s list of languages — or in English if neither is there. Once you choose a language, in Settings or with the EN / עב switch, it stays.
