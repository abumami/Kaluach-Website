Yom Ha’atzmaut

Yom Ha’atzmaut commemorates Israel’s declaration of independence on 5 Iyyar 5708, corresponding to 14 May 1948.

The national observance may be moved from 5 Iyyar to protect Shabbat and maintain its relationship with Yom HaZikaron, which immediately precedes it.

From remembrance to independence

Yom HaZikaron is observed on 4 Iyyar or near that date and is always placed immediately before Yom Ha’atzmaut. The sequence is intentional: it connects the sacrifice of those who fell in Israel’s defense with the establishment and continued existence of the State.

The transition at sunset from national remembrance to celebration can feel emotionally abrupt. That tension is part of the calendar’s meaning, holding grief, gratitude, and independence together rather than treating them as unrelated occasions.

Current calendar pattern

  • When 5 Iyyar would fall on Friday or Shabbat, Yom Ha’atzmaut is advanced to Thursday.
  • When the calendar sequence would place Yom HaZikaron on Sunday, Yom HaZikaron moves to Monday and Yom Ha’atzmaut to Tuesday.
  • Otherwise, Yom Ha’atzmaut is observed on 5 Iyyar.

Kaluach displays the officially designated date. Religious practice—including the form of the festive tefillah and Hallel—depends on community and rabbinic guidance.

Keeping the religious and civic celebrations together also preserves a certain national unity, whether the day centers on Hallel or the traditional Korban Mangal.
