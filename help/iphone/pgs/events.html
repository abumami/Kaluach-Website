iKaluach Help

Personal Events

iKaluach tracks recurring Hebrew dates and one-time events, with an optional civil-date recurrence and reminders.

Adding an event

Select the day in the calendar, then tap + — or touch and hold a day and choose New Event. The event’s Hebrew date is the day selected; choose the right day first.

Besides its name, an event can have a short Description — for example a Hebrew name, a relationship, or another note. It appears under the event in the selected day’s list and in All Events.

Event types

Azkara or yahrzeit: select the date of death. Choose the rite, Sephardi or Ashkenazi. For a death in Adar, choose which Adar to keep in a leap year — Sephardi practice keeps Adar II. For the first yahrzeit you may base the date on the burial instead: set how many days after the death the burial took place (1 to 30) with + and –, which shows the resulting Hebrew and civil date. The first yahrzeit is then kept on the burial date, and every later one on the date of death. Tap ⓘ for an explanation of each choice.

Birthday or anniversary: a plain-Adar birthday is treated as Adar II, with a reminder that opinions differ. A birthday records gender for its Bar or Bat Mitzvah date. A wedding anniversary can show a heart.

One-time event: one Hebrew date with no recurrence.

Every event needs a name. A date of death or birth can’t be in the future; the one exception is a Hebrew date that began at sunset this evening.

Optional civil recurrence

A recurring event may also recur on a fixed civil date. Because a Hebrew day touches two civil dates, choose either the earlier or the later one. This is in addition to the Hebrew recurrence.

Reminders

Turn on Remind me to be reminded the day before. See Alarms and reminders.

Finding and managing events

In the list below the grid, tap one of your events for its menu, touch and hold it to edit it, or swipe it left to delete it. The list button at the top opens All Events, with filters by type and a search field that finds events by name or description; tap an event there for the same menu. The menu can take you to the event’s original date, its burial date, its next Hebrew or civil occurrence, or a Bar or Bat Mitzvah date, and offers Edit and Delete. Deleting always asks first.

On your other devices

When you are signed into iCloud, your events appear in Kaluach Mac and on your other iPhones and iPads, and stay the same everywhere: an event added, changed, or deleted on one device follows on the others, usually within a minute. Nothing needs to be set up. Settings shows whether syncing is on; if iCloud is off for iKaluach, your events stay on this device.

Export and import

In Settings, under Events: Export Events… saves your events in a Kaluach events file — a backup, and the way to share events with family or a community, or to move them to Kaluach on Android or Windows. First it lists your events, all checked: tap the ones you don’t want to uncheck them. The type filters and the search narrow the list, and Select All and Select None act on the events shown — so to send only one family, search for its name and tap Select All. The count shows how many are chosen; Export… saves just those.

Import Events… reads such a file and lists its events the same way: new ones are checked, and events you already have are dimmed and marked “Already in Kaluach”. Import adds only the checked ones; importing never deletes or changes anything you have.

Export to Calendar (.ics)… lists your events the same way, then saves the next ten years of the checked ones as an .ics file for the Calendar app or another calendar app — every occurrence as an all-day entry, with its Hebrew date and description. This is one way only: iKaluach doesn’t read .ics files back, so keep the events file as your backup.
